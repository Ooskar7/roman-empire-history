# Romulus

## Biography

Romulus belonged to the legendary world through which Romans explained the origin of their city. Presented as the son of Mars and Rhea Silvia, he was raised outside formal power and returned as a figure of destiny, violence, and foundation.

His biography joins shepherd life, vengeance against Amulius, the quarrel with Remus, and the creation of Rome on the Palatine. Later tradition credited him with organizing the first Senate, dividing the people, and giving the new settlement its first military shape.

## Character and Public Image

Roman writers made Romulus energetic, severe, and intensely concerned with authority. He was not a gentle founder. His story gave Rome an origin in courage and conflict, but also in the belief that the city had to be defended even against family ties.

The ancient evidence for Romulus is uneven. Some details come from writers who lived much later, and some portraits were shaped by political admiration, moral judgment, or hostility. For that reason, this page treats the figure not only as a list of actions, but as a person remembered through Roman historical imagination. The dates usually given for this life are 771 BC to 717 BC, but for legendary or poorly documented figures the chronology should be read as traditional rather than certain.

## Historical Importance

Romulus became a model of beginnings: founder, warrior, lawgiver, and king. Whether historical or mythical, he remained a symbol for Roman identity because his life explained why Rome imagined itself as chosen, disciplined, and born from struggle.

Romulus is important to Roman history because the figure connects personal ambition, public duty, and the wider pressures of the age. The related events explain the battles, reforms, crises, or religious changes in more detail; this biography focuses on the person behind those developments, the social world that formed them, and the reputation they left behind. Through that reputation, later generations learned what Romans admired, feared, condemned, or hoped to preserve.
