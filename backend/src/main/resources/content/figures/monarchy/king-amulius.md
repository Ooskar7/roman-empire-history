# King Amulius

## Biography

Amulius appears in Roman tradition as a ruler of Alba Longa who seized power from his brother Numitor. His biography belongs to myth rather than secure history, but his role is important because Roman memory used him to represent illegitimate authority.

After taking the throne, Amulius tried to protect his rule by controlling Numitor family line. He forced Rhea Silvia into religious service and ordered the infant twins Romulus and Remus to be abandoned, hoping to prevent any future challenge to his kingship.

## Character and Public Image

The tradition presents Amulius as fearful rather than grand. His cruelty comes from insecurity, and his political imagination is limited to removing rivals. That makes him a useful contrast with the later Roman ideal of public authority restrained by custom and duty.

The ancient evidence for King Amulius is uneven. Some details come from writers who lived much later, and some portraits were shaped by political admiration, moral judgment, or hostility. For that reason, this page treats the figure not only as a list of actions, but as a person remembered through Roman historical imagination. The dates usually given for this life are Unknown to 754 BC, but for legendary or poorly documented figures the chronology should be read as traditional rather than certain.

## Historical Importance

Amulius mattered because his violence failed. The survival of the twins turned his attempt at dynastic control into the beginning of Rome foundation story. In Roman legend, tyrannical power could delay destiny, but it could not defeat it.

King Amulius is important to Roman history because the figure connects personal ambition, public duty, and the wider pressures of the age. The related events explain the battles, reforms, crises, or religious changes in more detail; this biography focuses on the person behind those developments, the social world that formed them, and the reputation they left behind. Through that reputation, later generations learned what Romans admired, feared, condemned, or hoped to preserve.
