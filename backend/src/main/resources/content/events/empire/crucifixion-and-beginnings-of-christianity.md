# The Crucifixion of Jesus and the Beginnings of Christianity (30-33 AD)

## Judea Under Roman Rule

Between about 30 and 33 AD, **Jesus of Nazareth** was crucified in Judea during the governorship of **Pontius Pilate**. At the time, Judea was a province under Roman authority, and Roman officials were concerned above all with public order, taxation, and preventing unrest.

Jesus preached within the Jewish religious world of the early first century. His teaching about the kingdom of God, repentance, mercy, and judgment attracted followers but also created tension with some local authorities. The Roman role in his death reflected the empire's power to punish those accused of disturbing public order or claiming dangerous authority.

## Crucifixion

Crucifixion was a Roman form of execution used especially for slaves, rebels, and those treated as threats to imperial order. The Gospels present Jesus as arrested in Jerusalem around Passover, brought before Jewish leaders, and then delivered to Pilate. The charge that mattered most politically was connected to kingship: the claim that he was "King of the Jews."

Pilate authorized the execution, and Jesus was crucified outside Jerusalem. For his followers, the death did not end the movement. They proclaimed that Jesus had risen from the dead and interpreted his crucifixion as central to God's plan of salvation.

## The First Christian Communities

The earliest Christians were Jews who believed Jesus was the Messiah. They gathered in Jerusalem, taught about his resurrection, prayed, shared meals, and baptized new believers. Over time, the movement spread beyond Judea through missionaries, trade routes, and urban networks across the Roman world.

Figures such as **Peter** and **Paul** became central to this expansion. Paul in particular argued that Gentiles could join the movement without becoming fully bound to every requirement of Jewish law. This helped Christianity spread among non-Jewish communities in the eastern Mediterranean and eventually in Rome itself.

## Historical Meaning

At first, Christianity was a small and vulnerable movement within the vast Roman Empire. Roman officials often saw it through local disputes or accusations of disloyalty, not as a world religion. Yet its message, organization, scriptures, and communities proved durable.

The crucifixion of Jesus became one of the most consequential events in Roman and world history. A provincial execution under imperial authority gave rise to a religious movement that would spread through the empire, face persecution, gain legal recognition, and eventually become central to Roman identity under later emperors.
