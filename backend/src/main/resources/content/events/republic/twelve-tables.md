# The Twelve Tables (c. 450 BC)

## Overview

The Twelve Tables were the first formal codification of Roman law, providing a foundation for the Roman legal system.

## Historical Context

Before the Twelve Tables, Roman law was largely unwritten and controlled by the patrician elite. This created tensions with the plebeians, who demanded transparency and fairness.

## Main Event

Around 450 BC, a special commission of ten men (the *Decemviri*) was appointed to write down the laws.

These laws were inscribed on twelve bronze tablets and publicly displayed in the Roman Forum so that all citizens could know their rights.

## Consequences

- Increased legal transparency
- Reduced abuse of power by elites
- Strengthened the rights of plebeians

## Legacy

The Twelve Tables became the basis of Roman law and influenced later legal systems in Western civilization.
