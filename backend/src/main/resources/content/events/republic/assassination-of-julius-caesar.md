# Assassination of Julius Caesar (44 BC)

## Overview

Julius Caesar was assassinated by a group of Roman senators who feared his growing power and the end of the Republic.

## Historical Context

After winning a civil war, Caesar became dictator for life. Many senators believed he aimed to become a king, which Romans strongly opposed.

## Main Event

On March 15, 44 BC (the Ides of March), Caesar was attacked during a Senate meeting.

A group of conspirators, including Brutus and Cassius, stabbed him to death.

## Consequences

- Political chaos and instability
- Renewed civil wars
- Rise of Octavian (Augustus)

## Legacy

Instead of saving the Republic, Caesar’s death accelerated its collapse and led to the creation of the Roman Empire.
