# Romulus and Remus Depose Amulius (754 BC)

## Mythological tradition

Before the legendary founding of Rome, the twins **Romulus and Remus** became involved in a struggle for power in the ancient Latin city of **Alba Longa**, a settlement traditionally considered the ancestor-city of Rome itself.

According to Roman tradition, Alba Longa had originally been ruled by the rightful king **Numitor**. However, his younger brother **Amulius** seized power through force and deception, overthrowing Numitor and declaring himself king. To secure his position and prevent any future heirs from challenging his rule, Amulius killed Numitor’s male descendants and forced Numitor’s daughter, **Rhea Silvia**, to become a Vestal Virgin, a priestess sworn to chastity.

Despite this, Rhea Silvia gave birth to twin sons: **Romulus and Remus**. Roman mythology claimed that the twins were fathered by the god **Mars**, giving them a divine origin that would later become central to Roman identity and propaganda. Fearing the threat posed by the children, Amulius ordered the twins to be abandoned and left to die near the River Tiber.

The twins survived. In one of the most famous legends of Roman history, they were discovered and suckled by a she-wolf before eventually being found and raised by the shepherd **Faustulus** and his wife. As they grew older, Romulus and Remus became strong leaders among local shepherds and villagers, gaining followers and influence throughout the region.

Eventually, the twins learned the truth about their birth and the injustice committed against their family. Determined to restore their grandfather’s rightful rule, they returned to Alba Longa and led a revolt against Amulius. The tyrant was overthrown and killed, while Numitor was restored as king of the city.

This event is deeply important in Roman tradition because it represents the beginning of the destiny of Romulus and Remus. By defeating Amulius, the twins not only avenged their family but also proved themselves worthy of leadership and divine favor. After restoring Numitor, they decided not to remain in Alba Longa. Instead, they chose to found a new city near the place where they had been saved as infants.

That decision would soon lead to one of the most famous moments in ancient mythology: the founding of Rome in 753 BC.

## Historical and Cultural Importance

Although modern historians consider the story largely mythical, it played a crucial role in Roman culture and identity. The tale emphasized several values admired by the Romans:

- The restoration of legitimate authority.
- Courage against tyranny and corruption.
- Divine protection and destiny.
- The heroic origins of Rome and its people.

The overthrow of Amulius therefore serves as the direct prelude to the foundation of Rome and the rise of one of history’s greatest civilizations.
