# The Founding of Rome (753 BC)

## Mythological Tradition

According to Roman tradition, the city of Rome was founded in 753 BC by **Romulus**, who became its first king. The story of Romulus and Remus became one of the most important foundation myths of Roman civilization and was deeply connected to Roman identity, politics, and religion.

The myth begins in the city of **Alba Longa**, where the twin brothers Romulus and Remus were born to **Rhea Silvia**, daughter of the rightful king Numitor. Their great-uncle **Amulius** had seized the throne illegally and feared that the twins could one day challenge his power. To eliminate the threat, he ordered the infants to be abandoned near the River Tiber.

Instead of dying, the twins survived. Roman legend tells that they were discovered and protected by a **she-wolf** in a cave known as the *Lupercal*. The animal nursed and cared for them until they were eventually found by the shepherd **Faustulus** and raised among shepherds and rural communities.

As adults, Romulus and Remus learned their true origins and overthrew Amulius, restoring their grandfather Numitor to the throne of Alba Longa. After this victory, the brothers decided to establish a new city near the place where they had been saved as children.

However, a dispute soon emerged between them. Romulus wanted to build the city on the **Palatine Hill**, while Remus preferred the **Aventine Hill**. To settle the conflict, they turned to the will of the gods through augury, the interpretation of signs sent by birds in the sky. Remus reportedly saw six birds first, but Romulus later saw twelve, leading both brothers to claim divine support.

The disagreement escalated into violence. According to the most famous version of the myth, Remus mocked the walls Romulus had begun building around the new city and leaped over them as an insult. In response, Romulus killed his brother, declaring that anyone who crossed the walls of Rome would suffer the same fate.

After the death of Remus, Romulus continued the construction of the city, which he named **Rome** after himself. He became its first king and established many of its earliest political, military, and religious institutions. Roman tradition also credited him with creating the Senate and organizing the first Roman army.

## Historical Meaning

Modern historians generally consider the story of Romulus and Remus to be legendary rather than historical fact. Nevertheless, the myth had enormous importance in Roman culture and served several symbolic purposes.

The story explained the divine origins of Rome through its connection to the god Mars, emphasizing strength, courage, and military power as central Roman virtues. The survival of the twins symbolized destiny and divine protection, suggesting that Rome itself was favored by the gods.

The conflict between Romulus and Remus also reflected deeper themes within Roman history: ambition, sacrifice, civil conflict, and the idea that greatness often emerged through struggle and violence. The killing of Remus became a symbolic reminder that the security and authority of Rome stood above personal bonds.

For centuries, Romans viewed this foundation myth not simply as a story, but as a reflection of the character and destiny of their civilization.
