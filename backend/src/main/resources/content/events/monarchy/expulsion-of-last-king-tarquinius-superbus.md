# The Expulsion of Tarquinius Superbus and the End of the Roman Monarchy (509 BC)

## The Fall of the Last King

According to Roman tradition, the monarchy of Rome came to an end in 509 BC with the expulsion of **Lucius Tarquinius Superbus**, known as **Tarquin the Proud**, the seventh and final king of Rome.

This event marked one of the most important turning points in Roman history: the collapse of royal rule and the birth of the **Roman Republic**. For later Romans, the overthrow of Tarquinius symbolized the rejection of tyranny and the defense of liberty, becoming a foundational moment in Roman political identity.

Tarquin had gained power through violence after the assassination of his predecessor, **Servius Tullius**, and Roman historians portrayed his reign as cruel, arrogant, and oppressive. Unlike earlier kings, who were traditionally presented as rulers working alongside the Senate and Roman institutions, Tarquin was accused of ruling through fear and personal authority.

Ancient sources claimed that he ignored the Senate, executed political opponents, and relied heavily on forced labor and intimidation. Although he expanded Roman influence and completed important construction projects such as the **Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus** on the Capitoline Hill, Roman tradition remembered him above all as a tyrant.

## The Story of Lucretia

The immediate cause of the revolution was linked to one of the most famous moral legends in Roman history: the story of **Lucretia**.

According to tradition, during a military campaign, several Roman nobles discussed whose wife was the most virtuous. When they unexpectedly visited their homes, Lucretia, the wife of the nobleman **Lucius Tarquinius Collatinus**, was found loyally managing her household, unlike the others who were attending banquets and festivities.

Her beauty and reputation deeply impressed **Sextus Tarquinius**, the king’s son. Some time later, Sextus visited Lucretia and threatened her, eventually assaulting her by force. Afterward, Lucretia summoned her husband and father and revealed what had happened. Although they assured her she was morally innocent, she declared that such dishonor could not remain unpunished and took her own life.

Roman tradition portrayed Lucretia as an ideal of virtue, honor, and loyalty. Her death shocked Roman society and became the catalyst for rebellion against the monarchy.

## The Revolution Against the Tarquins

Outrage over the actions of Sextus Tarquinius united many Roman nobles against the royal family. The rebellion was led by **Lucius Junius Brutus**, who swore an oath over Lucretia’s body to overthrow the monarchy and never allow kings to rule Rome again.

The Roman people and Senate turned against Tarquinius Superbus, who was eventually expelled from the city along with his family. Attempts by Tarquin to regain the throne through military alliances and foreign support ultimately failed.

With the monarchy abolished, Rome established a new political system: the **Roman Republic**. Instead of a king, supreme authority would now be shared between two annually elected magistrates known as **consuls**. According to Roman tradition, Lucius Junius Brutus became one of the first consuls of the Republic.

## Historical and Cultural Meaning

Modern historians debate how much of the traditional story is historical and how much was shaped by later Roman writers. Nevertheless, the expulsion of Tarquinius Superbus became one of the central political myths of Roman civilization.

The event symbolized the Roman hatred of tyranny and concentrated power. Throughout the centuries of the Republic, Romans frequently invoked the memory of Tarquin the Proud as a warning against kingship and dictatorship.

The story of Lucretia also reflected Roman ideals concerning honor, virtue, duty, and sacrifice for the good of the community. Together, these legends helped define the values that Romans believed distinguished their Republic from monarchy and despotism.

The year 509 BC therefore became, in Roman memory, the beginning of a new era: the rise of the Roman Republic, a political system that would dominate the Mediterranean world for centuries.
